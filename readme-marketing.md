## The problem (you know this one)

Every person on the team has their own pile of bookmarks.

- Dave has 14 folders and none of them make sense.
- Priya has the good link, but it is on her other laptop.
- The new hire asks, *"Which site do I use for that?"* for the fifth time.
- Nobody remembers where the monitoring page is. Everyone remembers that it exists.

Sound familiar? Then keep reading.

## The idea

**Keep all your useful tools in one place. Open any of them with two clicks.**

Web pages, internal sites, and even your own `.bat` scripts. All in one tidy Start menu, sorted into folders,
and every tool opens in a clean little window. No address bar. No 47 open tabs. No panic.

And yes, it looks like the **good old Windows menu**. Because clicking *Start > Programs* never gets old.

---

## What you get

### Looks great, works fast
- **Old-school Start menu** with folders that open to the side, just like the days when computers had personality.
- **Two retro looks.** Pick **Windows XP** (green Start button, blue sky) or **Windows 98 menu on a Windows 8 desktop**. Switch any time.
- **Desktop icons.** Right-click a tool, click *Add to desktop*, and it sits right there. Drag it where you want.
- **Find box.** Type two letters. Boom. Your tool is there.
- **Favorites.** The tools you use most show up on their own.
- **Notes with a log.** A tiny to-do list sits on your desktop. Click a note and an old-style window opens where you
  keep a log and change its status (To do, In progress, Waiting, Done). Perfect for "what was I doing on that BGP flap?"
- **Desktop clock.** The time, the day and the date, right in the corner. No more squinting at the taskbar.
- **Your own wallpaper.** Pick any picture as your desktop background. Yes, even that one of your cat.
- **Windows-style pop-ups.** Forms have a title bar you can drag and a close button, just like real windows.

### Does the boring stuff for you
- **Icons appear by themselves.** Add a link, and its logo is fetched for you. No design skills needed.
- **Pop-up windows with no browser clutter.** Each tool opens like a real app.
- **Runs your `.bat` files too.** Start a local tool, wait for it to be ready, and open its page. All with one click.
- **Right-click anything.** Edit it, move it, or delete it. No hunting through menus.
- **Drag and drop** a tool into another folder. That is it. That is the whole lesson.

### Safe and private
- **It lives on your own PC.** No cloud. No account. No tracking. No "we have updated our privacy policy" emails.
- **Backup in one click.** Download everything as a single ZIP. **Restore** it any time.
- **Factory reset** if you want a fresh start. (It asks you to type `RESET` first, so nobody wipes it by accident.)
- **Shut Down button.** It closes properly, like a grown-up program.

## Build it once. Share it with everyone.

This is the best part for teams:

1. One person sets up the folders and tools (or starts from the included starter pack).
2. Click **Backup (ZIP)**.
3. Send the ZIP to the team.
4. Everyone clicks **Restore (ZIP)**.

**Everyone now has the same tools, in the same folders.** No more "my list is different from yours."
Update the ZIP when something changes. That is all it takes.

*Small note: web links work on every PC. A `.bat` shortcut only works on PCs that have the same file in the same place.*

> **Plays nice with SharePoint and friends.** Keep your wiki and your documents where they are.
> Tools Manager is just the **fast door** to all of them. Think of it as the front door, not the whole house.

## Setup so easy, a kid can do it

1. **Download** the ZIP from GitHub and **Extract all**.
2. **Double-click `install.bat`.** Wait about one minute.
3. **Use the new Desktop icon.** Done!

No coding. No installing a pile of extra packages. It only needs **Python** (free) and **Windows with Edge or Chrome**.
If Python is missing, the installer tells you exactly what to do.

## Quick answers

**Is it a SharePoint killer?**
No. Please do not tell SharePoint. It is sensitive.

**Does it send my data anywhere?**
No. It runs on your own PC. Your list stays on your own PC.

**Why does it look like it is from 1998?**
Because the person who built it loves the old Windows menu. It is a feature, not a bug.

**What if I break it?**
Click **Factory reset**. Or restore your last backup. You did make a backup, right? *Right?*

**Is it free?**
Yes. Free to download and use, and the code is open source under the MIT license.
